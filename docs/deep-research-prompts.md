# Deep Research Prompts

These prompts are intended for ChatGPT Deep Research. Each asks for source-backed tables that can be converted into `config/research/*.csv`, `config/calibration-source-observations.csv`, and eventually validation-counted calibration rows. Require URLs, denominators, time periods, and construction notes before treating any result as empirical calibration.

## 1. Court-Specific Calibration Packs

Research empirical calibration targets for a comparative constitutional-review simulator covering the U.S. Supreme Court, German Federal Constitutional Court, French Constitutional Council, Supreme Court of Canada, Constitutional Court of South Africa, UK Supreme Court, India Supreme Court, Brazil STF, ECHR, and CJEU. For each system, find source-backed data from 2000-2025 where possible for: docket mix by doctrine, merits invalidation or rights-claim success, emergency/interim relief frequency, reason-giving and public disagreement in emergency or interim orders, intake/leave/complaint acceptance rate, time to review, public trust or institutional legitimacy, and compliance or implementation follow-up. Return a table with court/system, period, metric name, observed value, lower/upper plausible range, denominator, unit, source name, source URL, construction method, reliability rating, and whether the target is a direct analogue to simulator outputs or only contextual. Do not synthesize unverifiable numbers; mark gaps explicitly.

## 2. Legislative Response And Dialogic Review

Research how legislatures respond to constitutional-review outcomes under weak-form review, declarations of incompatibility, suspended declarations of invalidity, override clauses, pre-enactment review, abstract review, and mandatory response procedures. Prioritize the United Kingdom, New Zealand, Canada, South Africa, France, and Germany, with additional systems only when source quality is high. For each mechanism, report denominator-backed rates for legislative response, statutory amendment, good-faith cure, formal override, reenactment, nonresponse, and delay beyond a court-set deadline. Include case examples only after the quantitative table. Provide URLs, denominators, period coverage, coding rules, and warnings about comparability. Translate the findings into recommended simulator target rows for `legislativeResponseRate`, `legislativeResponseCredibility`, `suspendedDeclarationRate`, `overrideRate`, and `legislativeReenactmentRate`.

## 3. Compliance And Enforcement Channels

Research compliance with constitutional-court and supreme-court decisions across executive implementation, agency nonacquiescence, legislative reenactment, local-government compliance, direct defiance, workarounds, repeated litigation, and structural-remedy monitoring. Focus on the United States, Canada, Germany, South Africa, India, Colombia, the ECHR, and EU/CJEU contexts. Return a table of empirical measures with denominators, time windows, source URLs, and coding definitions. Separate formal compliance from timely implementation and from substantive policy cure. Identify which measures can calibrate `executiveImplementationRate`, `agencyNonacquiescenceRate`, `legislativeReenactmentRate`, `localGovernmentComplianceRate`, `defianceRate`, `workaroundRate`, `repeatedLitigationRate`, and `implementationCapacity`.

## 4. Legal-Transplant Feasibility And Political Culture

Research empirical and comparative-law evidence relevant to transplanting constitutional-review mechanisms across legal systems. Focus on legal tradition compatibility, constitutional-court familiarity, party fragmentation, governing coalition control, electoral time pressure, public trust, polarization, civil-society capacity, court capacity, and implementation capacity. Provide operational indicators, candidate datasets, source URLs, time periods, and warnings about causal interpretation. Return a mapping from each indicator to simulator fields: `legalTraditionCompatibility`, `partyFragmentation`, `governmentControl`, `electoralTimePressure`, `civilSocietyCapacity`, `implementationCapacity`, `publicTrust`, `partisanPressure`, `legalTransplantFeasibility`, and `politicalCultureSensitivity`. Include recommended ranges and whether each should be used as empirical calibration, sensitivity input, or only qualitative context.

## 5. Case Selection, Litigant Capacity, And Repeat-Player Advantage

Research empirical data on constitutional case selection and access to review. Cover discretionary certiorari/leave systems, constitutional complaint systems, constitutional councils, abstract review, ombudsman or rights-commission referrals, and public-interest or legal-aid litigation. Find evidence for intake acceptance rates, lower-court conflict generation, government win/loss posture, litigant capacity, public-interest support, amicus/NGO intervention, public defender or legal-aid participation, repeat-player government advantage, and time to final review. Return source-backed tables with observed values, denominators, source URLs, construction notes, and comparability warnings. Translate findings into candidate simulator target rows for `intakeAcceptanceRate`, `caseSelectionAccess`, `litigantCapacity`, `publicInterestSupport`, `governmentRepeatPlayerAdvantage`, `ombudsmanTriggerRate`, `publicDefenderParticipationRate`, `lowerCourtConflict`, and `averageTimeToReview`.

## 6. Source-Candidate Verification And Promotion

Audit the current source-candidate files for a comparative constitutional-review simulator: `legislative-response-source-candidates.csv`, `comparative-calibration-source-candidates.csv`, and `transplant-indicator-operationalization.csv`. For each candidate row, find the primary or most authoritative public source URL, verify the numerator, denominator, period, and coding definition, and decide whether the row can be promoted into a calibration-source-observation row. Return two tables. First, a row-by-row verification table with candidate file, row label, verified source name, source URL, verified numerator, verified denominator, verified observed value, period, coding notes, reliability, direct simulator analogue yes/no, and promote yes/no. Second, a ready-to-import CSV table using the schema `targetFile,profileKey,court,timePeriod,targetKey,label,lowerBound,upperBound,observedValue,n,unit,method,reliability,useForValidation,note,sourceName,sourceUrl,constructionNote`. Do not promote a row if the source is secondary only, the denominator is ambiguous, the metric is contextual rather than a simulator analogue, or the coding rule cannot be reproduced.
